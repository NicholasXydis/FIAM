# FIAM v4b — D21–D24 review and repairs

The strategy has **not** yet demonstrated profitability on the protected
2021–2026 evaluation, strong alpha in both legs, or the 12.8%/year target.
No full-window or OOS return was opened as part of D22–D24.
The final Modal suite passed **119 tests**, with 54 Pandas/Nautilus deprecation
warnings. These tests do not establish economic performance.

## What the additional work accomplished

D21 added uncalibrated engine beta, extra beta bands, and tighter style bands.
Its preregistered four-short-window results report pooled beta between −0.019
and +0.024 for the four E6 variants and significantly lower realized variance
than the reference. No E6 or reference variant passed all gates: individual leg
volatility intervals exceeded their preset limit, and one E6 formation failed
the first-rung condition. This was a risk study, not a profitability result;
costs, both-leg alpha and executed compliance remain unproved. W17 was already
viewed, and W17–W20 now are all viewed. See `DECISIONS.md` D21 and
`out/hbeta_gates/hbeta_gates.json`.

## Two validation defects found and repaired

1. **Daily versus holding-period risk.** G2 compared daily returns to
   `model.vol(snap,w)/sqrt(252)`, but the numerator is a multi-session horizon
   forecast with lagged covariance. `F1` also includes Newey–West autocovariance.
   They are not generally contemporaneous daily covariance. A point-in-time
   zero-lag factor covariance and `daily_variance`/`daily_vol` are now available,
   including VRA, PCA and specific variance. The optimizer's existing horizon
   risk and historical gates were not changed or re-run. Synthetic regression
   tests check the formulas and the cutoff.
2. **Short-cap floor.** The finals require each shorted company's market
   capitalization to be at least $500m at formation. The D19/D21 gate code
   mislabeled an aggregate short-notional threshold as that mandate. Formation
   gates now independently verify each short's dated `min(me, me_company)`,
   failing closed on absent values. The original aggregate short-notional
   requirement remains separately named `short_gross_research` for the
   registered experiments; it is not a finals mandate. Existing candidate
   selection also uses `short_ok`, but that is not a substitute for the gate.

## The risk interval needs more work

Prior to real-data re-gating, D22 tested the G2 90% chi-square volatility
interval on 10,000 independent simulated paths of 160 sessions. Coverage was
0.8993 for Gaussian returns and 0.6975 for standardized Student-t(5) returns;
only the Gaussian case met the predeclared [0.875, 0.925] requirement.

D23 tested percentile and basic-log bootstrap intervals, independently
preregistered. Their Student-t(5) coverages were 0.8293 and 0.8439, respectively;
both also failed the 0.875 minimum. Gaussian coverage was 0.8843 and 0.8882.
Neither interval is adopted. These are synthetic checks, not estimates of the
real-data interval coverage. The results are in `out/g2_known_truth.json` and
`out/g2_robust_known_truth.json`; the formulas and seeds are in the two scripts.

The D21 risk verdict is therefore **not promoted or reversed**. The fixed
daily-risk calculation has not yet been applied to historical returns, and no
valid robust replacement G2 interval has passed the registered checks. A
credible new interval also needs dependence and regime-change tests; iid
Student-t checks alone are insufficient.

## The return gate must report power

For perspective, suppose a book has 12% annualized daily volatility and 40
independent normal daily returns. A one-sided 5% mean-return test would need
roughly `(1.645 + 0.842) × 12% × sqrt(252 / 40) ≈ 75%` annualized mean return
for 80% power. A finite-sample t critical value makes the hurdle slightly
higher. This is an illustrative calculation, not a fitted result, a strategy
forecast, or a reason to loosen the user's two-month rule. It means a realistic
positive point estimate could be underpowered, and must then be reported as
such. A cross-sectional signal test may have different power, but dependence
between stocks and common shocks must be modeled rather than counting all
names as independent time observations.

## What remains before a profitability claim

- Correct the risk-model selection look-ahead documented in D25: the specific
  risk winner and factor half-life were selected using 2017–2020 outcomes and
  applied within that same window. This is selection contamination, not proof
  of how much the historical results changed. D26 implements and synthetically
  tests an as-of selector, but it has not been integrated into the book or
  evaluated on market data. Use pre-window or expanding selection with completed
  labels, and preserve the original results.
- Predeclare and validate a risk interval that meets coverage, boundary-size and
  power requirements under realistic dependence and stress. Then register how
  the corrected one-day risk will be assessed before opening any further data.
- Complete D21's execution audit, including the recorded E5 short-cap,
  participation, liquidation and data-gap exceptions, then obtain a clean G5
  executed-holdings smoke if the preceding gates are passed.
- Register and run separate long- and short-leg signal tests, with as-of data,
  beta adjustment, per-name costs and uncertainty. A risk-model pass alone
  cannot establish that either leg makes money.
- Only a candidate that earns the longer-window gate may use the D18 procedure.
  Report costs, both legs, beta, risk and breaches. Historical 2021–2026 was
  previously viewed and cannot be called a pristine holdout.
