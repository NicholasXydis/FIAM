## 1. Quantitative audit (recomputed, Dec-2020 formation unless stated)

Units established first, because every capacity number turns on them: `me` is USD **millions** ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:146)); `dolvol = prc × tvol` exactly (verified ratio = 1.0 on sampled rows) and is **monthly** dollar volume in USD. Implied ADV ≈ `dolvol / 21`. Anyone feeding `dolvol` to an optimizer as daily ADV overstates liquidity 21×. The plan warns against exactly this ("not monthly volume mislabeled as daily", plan §7:254) — **Keep**, and make it a unit test, not prose.

**Finding S4-01 — Fix, High.** Mandate §3.2:157 asserts "roughly 3,000 to 3,500 U.S. listed companies carry a market capitalisation above US$500m, so a 250-name short book is shorting something like seven or eight per cent of everything you are permitted to short." The plan repeats the mandate's framing without ever counting. Sampling intensity matters for the mandate's own argument (§3.2:158–160, alpha from selection within a heavily sampled pool) and for crowding/borrow: at 12% sampling the marginal short is much closer to "the average eligible name" than the deck will claim. Correct treatment: report the dated eligible count per formation month (it is also a required §9.4 exhibit input, mandate:986–987) and state sampling share against the *panel* pool, with the mandate's 3,000–3,500 noted as a listed-universe figure the panel does not reproduce. Unresolved: whether the gap is panel construction (CRSP/Compustat merge, `common`/`primary_sec` filters in [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:151)) or genuine — the team must not silently substitute one universe for the other.

**Finding S4-02 — Fix, Medium.** Plan §2:41 says "dated capitalization check" but never says *which* capitalization. The panel carries both `me` (security) and `me_company` (company) ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:146)). They disagree on the ≥$500m test for only 146 of 270k pooled 2021–2026 rows (0.05%), so the *count* impact is trivial — but the disagreement concentrates in multi-class issuers, exactly where borrow is security-level (float, lendable supply) not company-level. Mathematically correct rule: eligibility and borrow capacity on **security-level `me` (= prc × shares for that PERMNO)**, disclosed; `me_company ≥ 500` with a $60m traded class passes a company test while the actual short cannot be located in size. Verdict: specify `me`, log the 0.05% discordant set, never use `market_equity` characteristic (a transformed signal, one of the 147) as the compliance field.

**Finding S4-03 — Fix, High (daily formations).** The floor is "measured at the formation date" (mandate §3.1:120–121) and formations may now be daily (mandate §4:216). The panel gives `me` only at month-end (`eom` is a label, "not a separate publication timestamp", [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:137)). Plan §7 never says how a mid-month formation gets its market cap. The two lazy implementations both leak or breach: (i) month-end `me` applied mid-month uses the month-end price — future information; (ii) last month-end `me` can certify a name that has since fallen through $500m. Correct construction: `me_{i,d} = prc_{i,d} × shares_{i,last-known}` from daily `prc` in `dsf_mini_from_2014.csv` and the most recent *prior* month's `shares` (millions, [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:145)), with the staleness of `shares` disclosed, and eligibility re-checked on the execution date (close t+1), not only the signal date t. Plan §11:404 lists "treatment of formation-time short eligibility after passive cap drift" as a clarification — good — but the baseline cannot wait for the answer; adopt the conservative rule (a held short that breaches on *current* cap is flagged and covered on the next allowed execution, with the breach logged per plan §6:231, which is **Keep**).

**Finding S4-04 — Fix, Critical for the long leg.** The mandate's narrative (§3.3:189–192) and the plan's (§7:245) both frame capacity as a *short-leg* problem solved by the $500m floor. In this panel the floor does its job — short-eligible median ADV is 3–5× the all-names median — and the binding constraint flips: **an unrestricted long book at $6.67m/name needs a median 7.0 days at 10% participation, and 81% of names cannot take the position in one day at ≤10%.** The plan's liquidity machinery (§7:250–254, separate order/position capacity with `D_max`) is the right architecture — **Keep** — but §6's support selection ("rank by forecast advantage, existing holdings, tradability", plan §6:213) is not liquidity-first, and §2 imposes no long-side liquidity floor at all (correctly noting a price/ADV screen would be house policy, plan §2:49, then never setting one). Correct treatment: hard pre-filter *both* legs by lagged ADV before ranking (e.g. position ≤ `p·ADV·D_max` with p=10%, `D_max`=3–5), then rank within survivors; report how much forecast IC the filter destroys. Without this, the optimizer's "support-repair swaps" (§6:215) will be doing capacity triage on most of the book every rebalance, and the backtest will either infeasible-loop or quietly assume fills that violate its own participation rule.

**Finding S4-05 — Keep with fix, Medium.** Plan §6:214's internal bounds (min 0.10% = $1m, max 1% = $10m at inception) are honestly labeled "policy proposals, not validated optimum sizes." ### 1c. Short floor × compounding (mandate §3.3:182–187; plan §7:245, §2:35)

**Finding S4-06 — Keep the concern, Fix the quantification, High.** Plan §7:245 ("At $2bn NAV that requirement doubles") and mandate §3.3:183–185 ("success tightens your own capacity constraint") are directionally right and the plan's acceptance check "capacity is checked against compounded NAV" (§9:336) is **Keep**. Three corrections. (1) *Impact cost does not double when NAV doubles — participation does; cost grows as √NAV under the plan's own square-root law* (see S4-08): cost per rebalance as a fraction of NAV, `κσ|q|√(NAV|q|/ADV)`, scales as `NAV^{1/2}` at fixed weights `q`, so doubling NAV multiplies the *drag in return units* by √2 ≈ 1.41, while liquidation days `NAV|w|/(p·ADV)` scale linearly (×2.0). The plan conflates the two scalings in prose; the deck should state both, because they imply different mitigations (trade slower vs hold more names). (2) *The $500m floor is nominal and fixed*, so market-wide appreciation mechanically expands eligibility while compounding contracts capacity — in the data the eligible count is roughly flat (~2.05–2.1k, 2023–2026) because panel attrition offsets price growth, while short-eligible median ADV rises from $31.1m (Dec 2020) to $54.0m (Aug 2026). That nominal ADV growth *would* offset a doubling of NAV over this window — but booking that offset ex ante is assuming the market bails out your capacity. Correct treatment: capacity-test at compounded NAV against *lagged, stressed* ADV (plan §7:254 "stress it downward" — **Keep**, and specify the stress, e.g. ADV × 0.5, which is roughly a 2022-style volume/price drawdown for the median name). (3) *Gross is a choice*: mandate §3.1:116 caps gross at 200%, plan §2:38 correctly treats it as a ceiling. The compounding problem is partly self-inflicted if the book pins gross at 200% while NAV doubles; a capacity-aware policy de-grosses or adds names (toward 500) as NAV/ADV rises. The plan's fixed ~300-name support (§6:213) has no NAV-contingent breadth rule. Add one: `N_t` (or gross) adjusts so that median position days-to-trade stays ≤ `D_max`; report the rule, not ad-hoc changes.

Also note the interaction the plan *does* catch and should keep prominent: 300 names × 0.10% minimum ⇒ gross ≥ 30% (§6:217), so "de-risk to cash" breaches the 200-name floor — with capacity stress this becomes "forced holder of names you cannot exit inside `D_max`." The emergency hierarchy (§6:215) must rank: hard mandate floors (count, gross/net bands) vs house `D_max` — currently unspecified. **Fix, Medium.**

## 2. Cost model realism (plan §6:180–199, §7:258–267; mandate §4:234–240, §7:787–809)

**Finding S4-07 — Fix, Critical: there is no spread in the supplied data.** Plan §6:195 prices `Σ s_{i,t}|q_i|` with `s` a spread/commission, and §8:283 assigns CA0065S "price/volume history" to capacity/execution with gate "verify actual volume coverage, units, adjustments." Verified here: `chars` `bidask` is a 0/1 flag (median 0, max 1, 99th pct = 1) — not a spread; `dsf_mini_from_2014.csv` has exactly `permno, date, prc, daily_return` — **no volume, no quotes, no high/low**. So as supplied, (i) daily ADV does not exist outside Carbon Arc, (ii) quoted/effective spread does not exist at all, (iii) the only in-panel spread proxy is the characteristic `bidaskhl_21d` (a Corwin–Schultz-type high-low estimator among the 147, [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:162)), which is itself a *signal column* with its own availability timing. The plan never names this. Consequences: any `s_{i,t}` used before CA0065S access is invented; using `bidaskhl_21d` in both forecast and cost model is legitimate only if lagged consistently and disclosed (and it is a noisy, biased spread estimator for low-priced names — 25% of panel rows have `prc < $5.79`, where the plan proposes no price floor beyond house policy §2:49). Correct baseline until quotes are verified: model spread as a function of *lagged* price and ADV buckets (e.g. piecewise half-spread in bp calibrated to published effective-spread tables, cited and dated per mandate §7:797), with `bidaskhl_21d` as a cross-check, and show IR at 0.5×/1×/2× spread. Also state whether `s` is half-spread (correct for a one-way trade crossing from mid) or full spread — plan §6:195 is ambiguous and the error is a clean 2× on the linear term.

**Finding S4-08 — Fix, High: the square-root term as written mixes horizons and execution assumptions.** Plan §6:194–197:

`C_t(q) = Σ s_i|q_i| + Σ κ_i σ_{i,t} |q_i| √(NAV_t|q_i| / ADV_{i,t})`

with the honest caveat (§6:199) that coefficients must be calibrated/stressed — **Keep** the caveat, fix the formula's use. (a) *Units of σ.* The empirical square-root law (Almgren et al. 2005; Bouchaud et al., `impact ≈ Y·σ_daily·√(Q/V_daily)`, Y ≈ 0.3–1) takes **daily** volatility and the metaorder size `Q` executed against daily volume `V`. Plan §6 defines `Σ_{t,H}` and `μ_{t,H}` over horizon H and never states whether `σ_{i,t}` in the cost term is daily or H-day. Inserting H-day σ (≈ σ_daily·√H) overstates impact by √H (≈4.6× for H=21); inserting daily σ while `q` is in fact worked over `D` days (as §7's participation cap requires) overstates single-day impact because `Q` per day is `NAV|q|/D`. Mathematically consistent form for a trade worked evenly over D days at participation `p_d = NAV|q|/(D·ADV)`:

`ImpactCost_i ≈ |q_i| · Y · σ_{i,daily} · √(NAV|q_i| / (D·ADV_i))`  (+ a permanent-impact component that does *not* decay with D, if assumed).

Note cost then falls as `1/√D` — which is precisely the economic reason to trade slowly (Gârleanu–Pedersen, plan §12:447), and the plan's D-less formula *removes* that incentive from the optimizer while §7 imposes D as a separate constraint. Unify: let the optimizer see D (or participation) inside C, with `D_max` as the outer bound. (b) *Magnitude check the plan never does.* At Y=0.5, σ_daily=2%: participation 5%/10%/20%/42%/100% ⇒ impact ≈ 22/32/45/65/100 bp one-way. The *median long name* in §1b sits at ~42% one-day participation for a $4m position — i.e. ~65bp impact before spread, per trade, if executed in a day. At weekly rebalancing with 25% one-way NAV turnover/week and a blended 30bp all-in cost, drag ≈ 0.25×52×30bp ≈ **3.9%/yr of NAV**; the same turnover monthly ≈ 0.9%/yr. Against a cash+4% hurdle (mandate §3.4:206, ~4% in 2021), cost *frequency* is the strategy — exactly mandate §4:234–240's warning. The plan's "weekly review, daily exceptions" (§6:236–238) is defensible **only** with the no-trade/hysteresis machinery (§6:237, **Keep**) actually binding; require the E3/E4 ladder (§9:320–321) to report *realized* turnover and bp/trade, not just net IR. (c) `κ_i` per-name is unidentified from supplied data (no executions, no intraday). Use a single global Y with sector/vol buckets at most; per-name κ fitted on the test window would be hyperparameter leakage (mandate §7:727–730).

**Finding S4-09 — Fix, Critical: borrow is the largest single number in the cost stack and the plan leaves its definition open.** Plan §6:186 charges `H·Σ b_i·max(−w_i,0)/252` in the objective and §7:267 adopts the mandate's conservative alternative "50bp annual borrow for large caps and 5% for other eligible shorts, with a declared large-cap definition" (mandate §7:799–801) — **Keep** both, then close the definition. Arithmetic: dollar-neutral at 200% gross ⇒ short notional = 100% of NAV, so borrow drag = 1.00 × fee: **0.50%/yr if all "large", 2.75%/yr at a 50/50 mix, 5.00%/yr if none qualify.** At 100% gross (50% short) halve those: 0.25/1.38/2.50%. The spread between definitions (up to 4.5% of NAV/yr) exceeds the entire 4% benchmark premium. "Large cap" must be a dated, mechanical rule fixed before OOS (e.g. `me ≥ $10bn` at formation, or top-1000 by `me` that month — pick one, state the resulting short-book mix by year), applied to *security* `me` per S4-02. Further holes: (i) flat fees ignore utilization/hard-to-borrow spikes — the names a quality/issuance signal most wants to short are disproportionately expensive or unlocatable; at minimum stress the top-decile-conviction shorts at 10–25% and a locate-failure rate (position → 0, replaced, with the replacement cost shown). The plan lists "missing locates" as a stress (§7:267) — **Keep**, quantify it. (ii) Borrow accrues on *drifted* short market value daily, including recalls forcing covers at bad prices; §7's drifted-ledger rule (§7:275) covers accrual if implemented daily — state it. (iii) **Dividends on shorts**: see Hole H5 — if the return series is price-only, borrow-only accounting *understates* short cost by the dividend yield of the short book (~1–2%/yr on short notional for typical payers). (iv) Mandate §7:802–803 — fees never enter the forecast — plan §7:267 repeats it; **Keep**, and audit that no liquidity characteristic in the 147 (e.g. `dolvol_126d`, `ami_126d`) is serving as a disguised fitted fee curve chosen on OOS performance.

**Finding S4-10 — Keep, with one Fix (Medium): accounting identity.** Plan §7:258–265's ledger `R^NAV = r_f + Σ w_i r^e_i − TC − Borrow − FundingSpread` under a stated collateral convention, with explicit warnings against double-counting `r_f` and against charging borrow on gross/net, is correct and better than most first-round work the mandate describes. **Keep.** Fixes: (a) define `w` as *start-of-period drifted* weights and `TC` timing (charged at execution close t+1, reducing NAV before the t+1→t+2 return accrues) — off-by-one here silently shifts a day of returns; (b) the identity omits cash drag during the initial ramp and during multi-day executions (uninvested proceeds earn `r_f`, partially-filled targets earn partial alpha) — the simulator must carry share/cash state (plan §7:275, **Keep**) rather than weight-jumps, and the ramp convention (§7:256, **Keep**) must state whether formation costs for the initial book are charged inside OOS measurement; (c) financing spread on long debit balances when gross > collateral, and rebate haircut on short proceeds, are distinct terms — `FundingSpread` as a single plug will be challenged; split them even if one is set to zero with a stated reason (mandate §8.3:861–862, name what you did not model).

## 3. The information clock: is the plan's rule sufficient? (plan §7:269–275, §8:277–293; mandate §7:669–730, §6.5:551–609)

**Verdict: the stated rule is correct; the implementation surface is not closed.** Plan §7:271 restates the mandate exactly — information through trading day t forms the book at close t+1, first earned return close t+1 → close t+2, trading-calendar arithmetic, publication lag applied *before* the execution convention, conservative reading where lags might double-count (mandate §7:698–708). **Keep verbatim.** Plan §7:273 on Benzinga's two clocks (source T+1 governs historical eligibility, weekly delivery tested separately; CA0035 is metadata, no text) matches mandate §6.5:603–609 and §6.5:584–588. **Keep.** What follows are the holes *inside the supplied files* that the generic rule does not by itself close. Each needs an explicit dated rule + an assertion test of the kind mandate §8.1:829–832 requires (max feature date ≤ formation date, lag-adjusted) — the plan's acceptance list (§9:329–330) states the principle but enumerates none of these fields.

**Hole H1 — 8-K `filing_date` is an unverified provider date with a midnight placeholder (Critical for any daily text/event signal).** Verified: all 373,139 rows carry `filing_time_precision = 'date_or_midnight_placeholder'`; `filing_timestamp_utc` is 04:00 or 05:00 UTC only (= midnight ET under EST/EDT); `sec_accepted_at_utc` and `accession_number` are **100% null**; `provider_added_at_utc` is 100% null in this snapshot. [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:78) is explicit: `filing_date` is the "preserved provider filing date. Not independently verified against SEC acceptance records," and timestamps "must not be treated as actual release times" ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:82)). Plan §8:284 gates the CA0032 rebuild on "acceptance timestamps" but §7 gives **no safe-lag rule for the supplied corpus itself**. Safe rule (mathematically the only defensible one without acceptance times): treat the filing as information dated day t = `filing_date` *at unknown intraday time, possibly after the close*, hence under the mandate convention it may form a book no earlier than close t+1 earning t+1→t+2 — and because the provider date itself is unverified (ingestion could post-date acceptance, and there is no field to check), run the headline result with an **additional +1 trading-day buffer** (form close t+2, earn t+2→t+3) as the conservative case and report both. Never use: `content_report_date` as availability ("Not a public availability date", [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:79); empirically gap `filing − content` has median 0, 57% same-day, but ranges −358 to +1,708 days with 0.22% >30 days — using it back-dates information by construction); `filing_timestamp_utc` hour (placeholder); `period_end_date`, `provider_added_at_utc`, `retrieved_at_utc`, `processed_at_utc` ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:84)–87, all preparation clocks); `title_ai`, `sector`, `industry`, `metadata_json` as predictors ("not certified as historical, point-in-time predictors", [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:89)). Also handle multiplicity: up to 5 filings per PERMNO-day exist; aggregate to the formation cutoff before joining or returns duplicate ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:298)). On CA0032/EDGAR (mandate §6.4:530–532): acceptance datetime is the correct clock, mapping **through CIK with a dated CIK→PERMNO crosswalk** (mandate §6.4:536–538) — plan §8:284 says "historical CIK-security links," **Keep**, but add: EDGAR full-text availability can lag acceptance minutes–hours, and the crosswalk itself must be as-of (see H6).

**Hole H2 — Characteristics have no documented release lag or vintage (Critical).** Plan §7:271 says "adjust feature eligibility for its documented publication lag" — but for the 147 there is no documented lag to adjust for. [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:296) warns verbatim: "Merely matching months, or selecting a prior observation date, does not independently verify the release date of each underlying accounting value," and the audit "does not certify … original accounting vintages" ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:332)). (ii) Split assignment must key on **target month** (Jan-2021 target ⇔ Dec-2020 characteristics, [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:300)–304); keying on the characteristics row's `eom` leaks one month across every split boundary. **Hole H4 — Contemporaneous monthly aggregates used as daily inputs (High).** `dolvol`, `tvol`, `me`, `prc`, `shares`, `size_grp` on a month-t row summarize month t *including days after any mid-month formation*. Return definition: for IBM (PERMNO 12490) Jan–Mar 2021, `daily_return` equals the raw `prc` change to ~1e-16 *every day, including across its February ex-dividend date* — consistent with a **price-only return** (or a fully adjusted price series; the file alone cannot distinguish, and the mandate §11:1032–1036 does not define it). If `prc` is adjusted: `prc` is then not the traded price and must not be used for `me`/participation. Either way the plan (§8:282 "join on PERMNO and observation date; no forward-date join") is necessary but nowhere near sufficient. **Fix:** reconcile `dsf` against monthly `ret` (which [readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:138) defines as the stock-month return in USD) compounded within month, name the discrepancy, and if price-only, add a dividend adjustment (chars `div12m_me` is a characteristic, not a distribution file — source distributions via CA0065S/Arcana or state the omission and bound it: short-book dividend drag ≈ short-book yield × short notional). Also: merge discipline (mandate §11:1033 "do not merge on forward date") — as-of joins must be `feature_date ≤ t`, execution at t+1; a pandas `merge_asof` with default direction on unsorted data is the classic silent leak ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:298) "do not assume rows are sorted").

**Hole H6 — Identifier and label provenance (High for text/CA0035, Medium elsewhere).** The plan's instinct ("permanent identifiers", §2:34; "period-supported ticker/company labels", §11:400) is **Keep**, but four concrete traps in the supplied files are unnamed in §§7–8. **Hole H7 — Delisting, halts, missing days: principle kept, number missing (High).** Plan §7:275 ("missing data is not a zero return… handle delisting proceeds, bankruptcy stubs, halts explicitly; distinguish unknown from observed zero") and §9:334 are **Keep** and match mandate §7:721–723. What is missing is the *quantitative convention*. Facts: in `dsf`, "once a name delists it simply has no further daily return data" (mandate §11:1033–1034) — no delisting return is supplied; in chars, next-month outcomes are missing for ~0.4–0.6% of rows/yr (H3). If the simulator exits vanished shorts at the last price, it books the decline and never the stub — for distress delistings the delisting return is typically deeply negative for longs (hence *positive* for shorts if actually collectible, which it often is not once borrow is recalled and trading halts). Shumway (1997, plan §12:438) is the canonical reference; JKP's −30% imputation is a convention for *long* index returns, not a collectible short payoff. Mathematically honest treatment: (i) longs in vanished names: impute a delisting return with sensitivity (e.g. 0%, −30%, −55% for performance-related delistings per Shumway-type estimates — state source); (ii) shorts: assume cover at last traded price *only if* the name traded that day, else freeze at last price with borrow continuing until a stated resolution date, and never book a windfall from a −100% stub you could not have collected; (iii) report the count and NAV weight of affected positions per year. The plan must pick numbers before OOS; "handled explicitly" without numbers will read as the omission mandate §8.3:861 warns against.

**Hole H8 — Source/regime breaks inside the OOS window (Medium).** (a) All 28,453 chars rows in 2026 have `source_crsp = 0` (Compustat), whose returns are winsorized "using the pipeline's period-specific 0.1%/99.9% cutoffs" while CRSP values are unchanged ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:142)–143) — `ret_exc_wins` in 2026 is a *clipped* outcome; training/evaluating across the break without noting it understates 2026 tail losses. (Full-sample winsorization would also be a mandate §7:724–726 violation; period-specific within-date clipping is the supplier's choice — disclose, and do not add your own full-sample standardization on top; plan §9:307 within-date rule **Keep**.) (b) 2026 CUSIP/ticker evidence is continuity-inferred between Dec-2025 CRSP and Sep-2026 Compustat header snapshots ([readme.md](/home/drewclaw/Forkfiam/data/finals_2026/readme.md:103)) — identifier joins in 2026 carry look-ahead *in the mapping itself* (a Sep-2026 header confirming a Jan-2026 link). For PERMNO-native work this is harmless; for any CUSIP/ticker/CIK-joined external data in 2026 it is a real, if small, leak — flag the 2026 sub-period wherever such joins are used. (c) The benchmark: daily `b_d` uses `TB3MS_d` (plan §10:347–348; mandate §9.2:941–944) — monthly FRED values are revised and published with lag; using final-vintage TB3MS as a *feature* would leak (plan §10:351 already forbids it — **Keep**); as a benchmark it is prescribed, fine.

**Hole H9 — Weekly-delivery honesty for CA0035 (Medium).** Plan §7:273 keeps the mandate's two-clock allowance. Tighten: under source-publication timing an event dated t enters features t+1 and, via the execution convention, first earns t+1→t+2 *only if the book forms at close t+1* — teams routinely collapse this to "earn t+1" (mandate §7:704–708's spent-return error).
