## 1. Plan flaws all seven streams missed (Plan §§1, 6, 13, 14 re-read)

### M1. [HIGH] Fixed 4% premium makes headline IR increasing in scale; the §6 shrinkage stack optimises the wrong ratio

**Plan loci:** §1 table L25 ("Minimizing variance alone can deliver a tiny book that never clears cash + 4%"), §2 L38 (200% as "ceiling, not a target"), §6 objective L182–188, §14 L472–474. **Mandate loci:** benchmark cash+4% (§3.1 L127), IR as headline (§3.4 L210–211; §9.2 L941–945: active return `a=R-b`, IR = annualised mean(a)/sd(a)).

No stream derives what the fixed premium does to scale choice. Write NAV return as in Plan §7 (`R = rf + w'r^e - costs - borrow`) and benchmark `b = rf + 0.04` (annualised). Active mean is `E[a] = w'mu - C(w) - 0.04` and tracking error is `TE(w)=sqrt(w'Sigma w)` (benchmark is near-deterministic within month). For a scaled book `k·w` (k>0), ignoring cost convexity for one line:

`IR(k) = (k·m - C(k) - 0.04) / (k·s)`, where `m=w'mu-borrow(w)`, `s=TE(w)`.

As `k→0`, `IR→ -infinity` (the −4% drag over vanishing TE); as `k` grows, `IR` rises toward `(m - marginal cost)/s` until convex impact (`C ∝ k^{3/2}` dollars, Stream 06 §5) bends it down. **IR is therefore not scale-invariant, unlike Sharpe on excess returns.** Plan L25 states the intuition ("tiny book") but §6 then stacks four shrink-toward-zero devices — `gamma` risk aversion, `rho` SOC penalty, `eta||w||^2`, vol cap `sigma_max` (L208) — with no active-mean floor and no instruction to tune scale on IR. Tuning `gamma` on validation Sharpe (excess over cash, Plan §10 L353 correctly distinguishes it from IR) will systematically under-lever relative to the IR optimum and can turn a positive-alpha signal into a negative headline IR purely by shrinking it. Streams 01 (§1.3 KEEP ceiling), 03 (F8.3 vol 8–12% heuristic) and 04 (S4-06 "gross is a choice") each touch scale without this formula; 03's "IR≈0.5 needs ≈8% vol" is an expected-return statement, not the monotonicity result above.

**Mathematically correct alternative.** Choose gross/TE to maximise *net IR*, not Sharpe: on pre-OOS validation only, report the curve `IR(k)` for the frozen direction `w` with full convex costs and borrow, pick `k*`, and implement scale via a TE target (dual variable reported as implied `gamma`). Add a feasibility check: if `max_k E[a] ≤ 0` on validation, the correct product decision is the minimum-drag compliant book (see M4), not a shrunken alpha book that guarantees a large negative IR. Verdict on plan text: **KEEP L25 warning; FIX §6 to state the IR(k) problem explicitly.**

### M2. [HIGH] Two-stage sign preassignment is circular: optimal long/short sign depends on shadow prices unknown until Stage 2 solves

**Plan loci:** §4 forecast `mu_hat = x'theta` predicting *excess* return (L100–108), §6 Stage 1 "preassigned long/short signs" (L213), Stage 2 signed bounds (L214). **Mandate loci:** dollar neutrality as centre (§3.1 L118), shorts only ≥$500m (L120–121).

Every stream discusses signs only as hedge-vs-signal (03 F6) or calibration slope (02 F11). None states the centering problem. A pooled excess-return forecast is centred near the cross-sectional mean excess return (equity premium plus factor premia), not near zero. In a dollar-neutral book the relevant hurdle for name i is not `mu_i > 0` but, from the Stage-2 KKT conditions (schematically),

`mu_i - gamma·(Sigma w)_i - borrow_i·1{w_i<0} - lambda_net - beta_i·lambda_beta - (sector shadows)_i  ≷  ± s_i`,

where `lambda_net, lambda_beta` are the (unknown ex ante) shadow prices of the neutrality constraints. The lowest-`mu` names correctly shorted may still have `mu_i > 0` (expected standalone gain, relative loss); conversely a positive-`mu` high-beta name may belong short as the cheapest beta hedge. Fixing signs in Stage 1 by raw `mu` sign or rank therefore pre-empts the very prices Stage 2 exists to discover, and can force Stage 2 to hold names against their *shadow-adjusted* advantage for the whole backtest. The repo's incumbent does exactly this (signs fixed so gross is linear, verified at `fiam/book.py` solve constraints `side @ w == gross`, see §6 spot-check log) — a computational convenience the plan inherits without naming its cost.

**Correct alternative.** (a) Cross-sectionally centre forecasts before any sign logic: `mu_tilde_i = mu_hat_i - median_j(mu_hat_j)` (or liquidity-weighted mean) per formation date, and state that raw `mu_hat` levels never enter sign decisions. (b) Break circularity by iteration: solve Stage 2 once with free signs on an enlarged support (no min-size), read signs and shadow prices, then fix signs and re-solve with min/max bounds; log names whose free-sign and fixed-sign differ. (c) Rank Stage-1 candidates by shadow-adjusted advantage from the previous solve when available (incumbents credited saved exit cost, cf. 03 F6). Verdict: **FIX §6 Stage 1; current text as written will mis-sign the marginal third of the book.**

### M3. [HIGH] "Universal" pooled ridge assumes homogeneous theta; no stream requires the heterogeneity test that could falsify it

**Plan loci:** §4 title "universal forecasting component" (L96), pooled loss with single `theta` (L102–108), sector *controls* (intercept shifts) only (L110), §1 L11 ("shares statistical strength"). **Mandate loci:** none requires universality; §11 lens 1 (L1090–1094) will punish a pooled fit whose apparent alpha is composition.

A single `theta` imposes identical characteristic premia for mega-caps and microcaps, financials and tech. If true premia differ by size or sector — the central empirical lesson of HXZ size splits and GKX interactions, both verified by Stream 02 (§3.1, F8) — pooled ridge estimates a variance-weighted average premium that can have the wrong magnitude, and in principle the wrong sign, inside the *tradable* subset (≥$500m shorts, ADV-feasible longs) the fund actually holds. Sector dummies do not fix this: they shift intercepts, not slopes. Streams 02/04 correctly demand tradable-universe training and leg-specific ICs, but neither demands a slope-stability test, so a rewrite could satisfy both streams and still ship a homogeneous model on a heterogeneous cross-section.

**Correct alternative (cheap, pre-register in E-ladder).** On validation folds only: fit ridge separately on large vs small (lagged `me` median split within tradable universe) and report `theta` correlation and per-family sign agreement; equivalently test interaction ridge `mu = x'theta + (x·z_size)'delta` with `delta` under the same df budget (02 F3). Promote interactions only on net-IR improvement. If slopes are stable, pooling is vindicated with evidence; if not, the "universal" claim in the deck must be withdrawn before Arcana sector/size attribution withdraws it. Verdict: **FIX Plan §4 — add heterogeneity gate; do not assert universality.**

### E1. [MEDIUM] Stream 02 decay table (§6.1): two of three "share in first 5d" entries are wrong, one is impossible

02 defines cumulative alpha `A(H)=mu(0)(1-e^{-kappa H})/kappa`, so share of 21d alpha earned in first 5d is `(1-e^{-5k})/(1-e^{-21k})`, `k=ln2/h`. Recomputed:

| half-life h | 02 table | Correct | 02 "remaining at day 10" | What that column actually is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3d | 69% | **69.0% ✓** | 10% | instantaneous `e^{-10k}`=9.9% ✓ |
| 15d | 26% | **33.2% ✗** | 63% | `e^{-10k}`=63.0% ✓ |
| 60d | 22% | **26.1% ✗** | 89% | `e^{-10k}`=89.1% ✓ |

The 60d entry (22%) is below the `h→∞` limit `5/21=23.8%`, which the ratio approaches monotonically from above — impossible under 02's own formula. The table also mixes two objects without labelling: column 1 is a cumulative share, column 2 is instantaneous decay `e^{-ka}` (not "share remaining" of the 21d total, which for h=15 at a=10 would be `(e^{-10k}-e^{-21k})/(1-e^{-21k})≈40%`). **Consequence:** qualitative verdicts (fast ⇒ daily matters, slow ⇒ weekly≈monthly) survive; any sizing that used 26%/22% understates early alpha capture for slow signals by 4–7pp. Correct the table, label both columns with formulas, and note the GP aim-weight mapping (02 §6.1 two-knob discussion) is unaffected qualitatively. 07's GP aim sketch (`aim ∝ Σ(…)`, §3.5) contains literal ellipses in place of the discount weights — not an arithmetic error but an incomplete formula that must not be presented as the GP solution (03 F5.1's cost-model table is the correct substitute).
