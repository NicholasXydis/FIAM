## 1. Validation design (Plan §9 L303–340 vs Mandate §§5.2, 7, 8.1)

### 1.1 [KEEP] Walk-forward skeleton and researcher-contamination disclosure

Plan §9 L305 adopts the mandate's own default — train 2015–18 / validate 2019–20 / first test 2021, then roll annually (Mandate §8.1 L824–826) — and correctly refuses the fiction of a single "2015–2020 train vs 2021–2026 OOS" split: a single frozen 2015–20 fit judged on 2021–26 would conflate model *ageing* with model *quality* and waste five years of legitimately available refits. Annual expanding/rolling refit "with training through Y−3 and validation Y−2 through Y−1" (L305) plus "all labels must have matured before a refit" is the right shape. L309 ("already examined 2021–2026 … does not make it an untouched research holdout … label it a historically walk-forward evaluation with prior researcher exposure") is exemplary and directly answers Mandate §5.2's demand to "hold out a slice of data that nothing touched until the very end" (L356–358) — the plan honestly states no such slice exists, rather than renaming 2025–26 a holdout. Keep both; the deck must carry the L309 sentence verbatim or judges will extract it under questioning (Mandate §8.3 L858–860, §10 item 8 L1020–1022).

One ambiguity to freeze now, not at the event: L305 leaves open whether the window *expands* or *rolls* after the first split, and whether "refit on eligible labelled observations through Y−1" (i.e. refit on train+validation after selection) is used. Mandate §8.1 L825–826 says "expand the training window and roll the validation window" — pick that. If you refit on the validation years after choosing hyperparameters on them, the validation metrics no longer describe the deployed model; that refit is legitimate only if predeclared (Plan says so) **and** the deck labels validation numbers as selection-stage, not as unbiased estimates of the refit model's skill. Verdict: **KEEP with this one-sentence FIX.**

### 1.2 [CRITICAL — FIX] Purge without embargo is insufficient for overlapping 21-day labels at weekly/daily decision frequency

Plan §9 L305 requires only: "Purge observations whose target horizons cross a split boundary." No embargo is specified anywhere (verified: no occurrence of `embargo` in the plan). With H≈21-trading-day labels (Plan §4 L114) and weekly reviews (§6 L236) / daily features, purging removes train rows whose label windows overlap the test start, but test-period features still share underlying return days with late-training labels through serial dependence in features (trailing volatility, momentum, EWMA risk §5 L154) — the classic leakage channel purged-CV alone does not close (López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, 2018, Ch.7: purge **and** embargo; embargo ≈ 1–5% of sample or ≥ label horizon). The mandate does not use the word "embargo" either, but §7 items 1–2 (L698–708, "earning a return your signal already used") and §7.8 (L727–730, hyperparameters tuned on test) make the substance a disqualification risk, and Plan §9's own acceptance check "no feature … available after its simulated decision time" (L329) is untestable without a stated gap.

**Correct alternative:** embargo of at least H trading days (21) after every train/validation and validation/test boundary, in addition to purging overlapping labels; i.e. for test year Y with a 21-day label, the last usable training label must *mature* ≥21 trading days before the first formation date of Y. Cost is trivial (loses ~1 month of labels per split); omitting it inflates validation IC by letting near-boundary training labels share return days with early-test features.

### 1.3 [CRITICAL — FIX] No statistically meaningful acceptance thresholds: 68 months cannot support the gates the plan implies

Plan §9 L340 says "Freeze any required validation IR improvement, beta band, stress budget and drawdown tolerance before running the final comparison" — then freezes none of them. The "minimum acceptance checks" (L329–338) are ten binary *process* checks (timing, reconciliation, no tiny-weight padding) and zero *statistical* checks. Plan §4 L120–121 ("promotion requires better net portfolio economics … compare rank IC, net IR…") likewise gives no magnitude. This is the plan's largest validation defect, because with only 68 monthly OOS observations (L311) almost any plausible threshold the team invents mid-event will be either unpassable or meaningless. The numbers, computed for this review (monthly frequency, T=68):

- An annualised IR of **1.0** is a monthly Sharpe of 0.289, giving t = 0.289·√68 = **2.38**. IR 0.5 gives t=1.19 — indistinguishable from zero at any conventional level. The headline cash+4% hurdle (Mandate §9.2 L944–945, "the headline number") will therefore be *reported*, but cannot be *statistically established* in this window; the deck must say so rather than imply a passed gate.
- A mean rank-IC of 0.02 with cross-sectional IC s.d. 0.08 gives a naive t of 2.06 over 68 months — and that naive t is itself overstated under overlapping 21-day labels evaluated weekly, where the effective number of independent observations is ≈1,420/21 ≈ **67**, not the ~290 weekly rebalances. Any IC t-stat must use HAC/Newey–West (Plan §12 L439 cites Newey–West but §9/§10 never commit to a lag) with lag ≥ H, or non-overlapping monthly ICs.
- Harvey–Liu–Zhu (2016, cited Plan §12 L430) argue a factor "discovery" needs t ≳ 3.0 under multiple testing, not 2.0. From the line above, that requires mean IC ≈ 0.03 at s.d. 0.08 sustained for 68 months — a demanding but honest bar the plan should adopt explicitly for any E5 incremental-feature claim.

**Correct alternative (freeze these before any OOS run):** (a) promotion at each ladder step requires Δ(net IR) > 0 with a paired block-bootstrap 90% CI excluding 0 on *pre-OOS validation only* (Plan L325 already restricts promotion to pre-OOS — keep); (b) report the Probabilistic/Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) for the final strategy: DSR = Φ[(ŜR−SR₀)√(T−1)/√(1−γ₃ŜR+(γ₄−1)ŜR²/4)], where SR₀ is the expected maximum Sharpe across N trials under the null; (c) require IC HAC-t ≥ 3 for any "incremental information" claim. If the team cannot pass these, the correct deck language is "point estimate positive, statistically unresolved at T=68" — Mandate §8.3 rewards exactly that honesty.

### 1.4 [CRITICAL — FIX] Effective number of trials is never defined; the E0–E6 ladder understates it by an order of magnitude

Plan §9 L311 requires logging "every tested family, feature, hyperparameter configuration and discarded experiment" and a "dependence-aware multiple-testing procedure" — but names no procedure and never converts the log into an N that deflates anything. The ladder (L313–325) has 7 headline steps, yet each step embeds dozens of implicit trials (ridge λ grid, tree depth, support size, participation rate, beta band — §§4–7 candidate values). Under Bailey–López de Prado's expected-maximum-Sharpe benchmark at T=68 months, computed for this review assuming independent trials (an upper bound; correlated trials deflate less, which is why N must be *effective* N):

| Trials N | E[max annualised Sharpe \| zero skill] |
|---|---|
| 7 (ladder headlines only) | 0.59 |
| 20 | 0.80 |
| 50 | 0.96 |
| 100 | 1.07 |
| 400 (Mandate §5.2's agent-proposes-400 scenario, L354) | 1.26 |

Read: if the team (or its §5.2-style exploration agent) effectively tries ~100 configurations, an observed OOS Sharpe/IR of ~1.0 is the *expected maximum of pure noise*. Reporting it as skill without the denominator is precisely what Mandate §5.2 L356–360 says judges will interrogate ("state how many hypotheses you tested, in total … we will ask for the denominator") and what Harvey et al. and Feng–Giglio–Xiu (Plan §12 L430, L433) formalise. Bayesian shrinkage (Plan §3) does not fix this — Plan L311 itself concedes "Bayesian shrinkage does not erase the selection bias" — yet no downstream correction is specified.

**Correct alternative:** (i) pre-register an experiment registry whose count *is* N (count hyperparameter configurations, not ladder steps; collapse near-duplicate configs by return-correlation clustering to an effective N, e.g. López de Prado's ONC clustering); (ii) report DSR (§1.3) with that N and the cross-trial Sharpe variance; (iii) as a complement, report PBO via combinatorially-symmetric CV (Bailey–Borwein–López de Prado–Zhu 2014) on the validation-stage candidates — with only ~6 pre-OOS years PBO will be coarse (few CSCV partitions), so present it as a diagnostic, not a second gate. PBO near 0.5 = selection is coin-flip OOS; the worked example in that paper shows PBO 55% coexisting with a highly "significant" in-sample Sharpe, which is exactly the trap E0–E6 sequential promotion sets.

### 1.5 [HIGH — FIX] Ladder discipline: sequential one-change-at-a-time promotion on one validation set is adaptive overfitting

E0→E6 (L317–323) changes one major component at a time and promotes on pre-OOS validation (L325). That is better than a combinatorial sweep (L325 forbids "untracked combinatorial sweep") but still adaptive: each step's winner becomes the next step's baseline, so validation is reused ≥7 times (plus within-step tuning), and validation performance of the final E6 survivor is biased upward even if every individual comparison was honest. With validation = 2019–20 (24 months, dominated by COVID), there is an additional regime risk: choices that "win" 2019–20 (e.g. faster risk estimator §5 L154, weekly review E4) may be COVID-specific. Fixes: (a) nest the ladder — select within-step settings on 2019, confirm the step on 2020 (or vice versa), never both jobs on both years; (b) carry a *validation-reuse count* into the DSR N of §1.4; (c) for regime robustness, report each promoted step's validation ΔIR split 2019 vs 2020 separately — a step that wins only in Mar–Apr 2020 is a crisis-hedge claim, not a general improvement, and must be labelled as such (cf. Mandate §11 lens 6–7 on earnings-window/stress concentration, L1108–1114).

### 1.6 [KEEP] Dependence-aware uncertainty, with one specification gap

"Maintain time blocks when estimating uncertainty — random stock-row splits are invalid" (L307), date-balanced loss (§4 L110), within-date normalisation only (L307; Mandate §7.7 L724–726), and block-bootstrap intervals (L311) are all correct — keep. Gap: block length is never stated. For 21-day overlapping labels use a moving/circular block bootstrap with block ≥ 21 trading days (≈1 month; Politis–Romano stationary bootstrap with mean block 21–42 is acceptable) and state it; a 5-day block would understate every CI in §1.3. Verdict: **KEEP + one-line FIX.**

### 1.7 [MEDIUM — FIX] "Train 2015–2020" data reality: 2015 rows are thinner than the split implies

The mandate supplies daily returns from 2014 (Mandate §11 L1032) and the panel from 2015, so a 2015 training start means: 252-day beta/vol windows (Plan §5 L154) are unavailable until 2016, 12-month momentum characteristics are left-censored in 2015, and Carbon Arc assets starting 2019 give 2 pre-2021 years (Mandate §6.2.1 L443–446). Plan §5 L156 handles short-history names (shrunk sector priors) but §9 never restricts *which* hypotheses are testable from 2015 vs from 2017/2019. Each ladder step must state its earliest honest formation date; an E5 feature with 2019-start data validated on "2019–2020" has effectively zero training years and must enter as a separately-reported subperiod sleeve (Mandate §6.6 L620–623; Plan §8 L293 agrees — enforce it in the ladder, not just the data table).

## 2. Reporting compliance (Plan §10 L342–361 vs Mandate §9 L863–988, §11 L1088–1151)

### 2.1 [KEEP — exemplary] Benchmark, Sharpe-vs-IR, and annualisation conventions

Plan §10 L344–353 transcribes the prescribed benchmark exactly — b_m = TB3MS/1200 + 0.04/12, b_d = TB3MS/25200 + 0.04/252 (Mandate §9.2 L941–943) — and gets three distinctions right that most teams will get wrong: (i) daily-compounded and monthly benchmark formulas will not reconcile and the gap must be shown, not silently fixed (L351); (ii) Sharpe is excess over cash, IR is active over cash+4% — "Sharpe uses excess returns over cash, not over cash + 4%" (L353), with IR = √K·mean(a)/sd(a), a = R−b; (iii) CAGR from ending NAV vs arithmetic annualised mean as the ratio input, unreconciled by design (L353; Mandate §9.2 L931–938). One addition: Mandate §9.2 L943–944 says a supplied benchmark series governs ("so that every team is measured against identical numbers"); Plan L43/L351 says "use the supplied series when available; implement independently for reconciliation" — keep, and in the deck footnote the supplied series as primary. If the supplied series and your TB3MS formula differ (discount-basis vs investment-basis, holiday alignment), the supplied one wins for the headline IR.

### 2.2 [KEEP with MEDIUM FIX] §9.1 table coverage: complete on strategy rows, silent on the benchmark column

Plan §10 L357 enumerates every §9.1 strategy row (Mandate L874–924): ending endowment, cumulative, annualised arithmetic + CAGR, volatility, alpha±s.e., daily/monthly beta±s.e., Sharpe, IR, tracking error, max drawdown, worst day/month (= Mandate "maximum one-month/one-day loss" L898–900), avg/min/max holdings, gross/net ranges, turnover, holding period, average $ position, median short market cap, annual trading + borrow costs, net-of-all-costs IR. Nothing is missing. Two wording fixes before it becomes code: (a) Mandate requires the table "for the strategy **and for the benchmark where applicable**" (L867–868) on **one slide** (L867) — Plan never mentions the benchmark column or the one-slide constraint; benchmark cumulative/annualised/volatility/Sharpe/max-drawdown must be computed on the supplied b series (a near-cash series: its "Sharpe" vs itself is degenerate — report return/vol/drawdown for the benchmark and n/a elsewhere exactly as the mandate's n/a column does, L884–924). (b) Gross is "average/maximum" and net "average/min/max" (L904–908) — Plan's "ranges" (L357) must not collapse gross to min/max only. Verdict: **KEEP, two one-line FIXes.**

### 2.3 [KEEP with HIGH FIX] Turnover: formula correct; annualisation under weekly-core + daily-exceptions is undefined by the prescribed multiplier

Plan §10 L355 states the mandated one-way definition exactly — Turnover(τ) = ½Σᵢ|wᵢ,τ − wᵢ,τ−| with w− the *drifted* pre-trade book (Mandate §9.3 L959–961; drift requirement Plan §7 L275, "never rebalance for free by resetting yesterday's weights") — and correctly warns that holding period is not 1/turnover at 200% gross (Mandate L963–967: full annual replacement at 200% gross = 200% one-way turnover; Plan L355 "do not claim leverage cancels out"). Holding period must be measured directly (position-weighted days in book) or labelled gross/turnover approximation. All keep.

The gap: Mandate §9.3 L961–963 annualises as *average per-rebalance × rebalances/year* (252/52/12). The plan's production cadence — weekly core review plus irregular daily risk-exception trades (§1 L9, §6 L236–238) — has no single "rebalances per year": multiplying a weekly average by 52 while exception trades ride free understates annual turnover (and costs); counting every exception as a "rebalance" and multiplying by ~252 overstates it. Plan L355 half-sees this ("for irregular exception trades, also report total turnover divided by elapsed years and explicitly define which dates count") but presents the honest figure as the *supplement*. **Fix (invert the hierarchy):** headline annualised turnover = Στ Turnover(τ) / elapsed years over *all* trade dates (core + exceptions + forced repairs), with the mandate's average×frequency figure shown for the core schedule as the reconciliation; state the trade-date definition on the §9.1 slide. This matters beyond cosmetics: at $1bn the cost ledger (§7) multiplies turnover by spread+impact, so an understated turnover definition directly overstates net IR — Mandate §4 L234–237 makes turnover "the binding constraint … expect the judges to ask."

### 2.4 [KEEP with MEDIUM FIX] Beta/alpha regressions: right regression, unspecified standard errors and annualisation of alpha

Plan §10 L353: "Alpha/beta regressions use portfolio and S&P 500 excess returns; report robust standard errors and the window" — matches Mandate §9.2 L947–951 (excess-on-excess, s.e. on both, daily **and** monthly because "a book that looks neutral monthly can carry meaningful daily beta, and vice versa"). Plan §5 L168 adds the key honesty point ("an insignificant beta with a wide confidence interval is weak evidence of neutrality"). Keep. Specify before coding: (i) "robust" must mean HAC/Newey–West with a stated lag (daily: ≥5–21 lags given weekly rebalancing induces return autocorrelation; monthly T=68: 3–6 lags) — plain White s.e. under autocorrelated daily active returns will understate beta s.e. and flatter neutrality; (ii) alpha annualisation is *arithmetic* (×252 daily, ×12 monthly, Mandate L947–948), so daily-alpha×252 and monthly-alpha×12 will differ — report the monthly as headline-adjacent and daily as the compliance check, not two competing alphas; (iii) daily beta on a book with illiquid/small-cap names is biased toward zero by nonsynchronous trading — if daily beta ≈ 0 but monthly beta ≠ 0, the correct interpretation is stale-price attenuation (Dimson betas with leads/lags as sensitivity), not "neutral daily." Plan's demanded 252-day rolling beta exhibit (L359 = Mandate §9.4 L980) must use the same excess-return definition; label the first 252 days unavailable rather than backfilling (Plan L359 already requires honest labelling — keep).

### 2.5 [KEEP] Gross vs net of costs

Plan requires gross/net throughout: costs modelled as separate trading/borrow/financing lines (§7 L258–267, incl. the mandate's conservative 50bp large-cap / 5% other borrow alternative, Mandate §7 L799–801), yearly gross/net returns exhibit (L359 = Mandate §9.4 L981), and net-of-all-costs IR as the §9.1 closer (L357 = L924). ## 4. The single-agent proposal (Plan §8 L295–301 vs Mandate §5 L254–396, §7 model-memory L733–783, §8.2)

### 4.1 [HIGH — FIX] The narrow role is safe but mis-aimed: it defaults to the lowest-value §5.1 role and omits §5.2 entirely

Mandate §5 offers six risk-layer roles (L278–305) and calls **context-conditional position sizing** (#3, L288–293: smaller into earnings prints without edge, tight borrow, multi-day exit, crowded consensus) "the single highest-value application available to you," with pre-trade constraint *explanation* (#1) and post-trade explainer (#6) as reason-giving, not return-changing, roles. Plan §8 defaults to exactly #1/#6 ("exception and pretrade explainer") and relegates action-taking to a three-item menu (maintain / reduce flagged exposure / defer trade, L299). Consequences:

- An explainer-only agent **cannot pass Mandate §5.3.4** (build and report the with/without counterfactual, L379–382; repeated as deck item 5, L1013–1016) in any informative way: by construction Δholdings = 0, so the counterfactual is identical and the "agent evidence" slide is empty. Plan L301 concedes this ("do not claim an explanatory-only agent improved returns … value … in error detection and faithful explanations") — honest, but Mandate §5 L393–396 is explicit that a *measured negative* action-taking result earns credit while "an agent in the architecture diagram with no evidence that it changed anything" earns nothing. An explainer that once caught a real breach (with the log) can still score under #5 anomaly-escalation logic, but only with worked examples — Plan should name that as the explainer's success metric (breaches caught / false alarms on validation rebalances), not explanation quality in the abstract.
- The action menu (maintain/reduce/defer) is coarser than §5.1.3 sizing and than §5.1.4 trade triage (rank-noise trades skipped against the cost model, L294–297), both of which map directly onto quantities the deterministic layer already computes (days-to-exit §7 L251, borrow bᵢ §6 L186, marginal cost of q). The mathematically clean action-taking form the plan misses: agent outputs a bounded multiplier m ∈ [0,1] (or a defer flag) per *exception-flagged* name/trade, code applies m to the optimizer's target trade and re-solves/validates all hard constraints — preserving "agent proposes; code enforces" (§5.3.1 L369–372) while making the counterfactual (m≡1) non-trivial and the IR attribution well-defined.
- **§5.2 (upstream exploration: dataset triage, hypothesis generation with mandatory ex-ante rationale, cross-dataset joins, documentation archaeology; L308–342)** is half of "this year's differentiation" (L260–263) and Plan §8 never mentions it — §8 admits alt data only as "at most one incremental hypothesis" chosen by humans. Given Carbon Arc's REST/MCP access (L314–320, 20K tokens/day cap) and the workbook already doing most triage, a bounded §5.2 agent (triage + documentation archaeology over the 15-asset starter list, producing the four-question screen §6.6 as a deck artefact) is cheap, safe (no trade impact), and directly scored. **Fix:** two-agent-role design from one model — (a) exploration/triage agent pre-event, denominator-logged (§1.4); (b) exception-triggered sizing/triage agent in the risk layer with bounded multipliers. If time forces one, (b)-with-counterfactual outscores the pure explainer; the explainer becomes (b)'s logging format, not a separate agent.

### 4.2 [KEEP] Point-in-time and model-memory controls are correctly specified

Plan §8 L301 implements Mandate §§7–8.2 faithfully: no live retrieval for historical prompts (§8.2.1 L840–842), declared release/cutoff dates (§8.2.3 L845–848), measured re-identification rate on blinded text (§7 L765–769: "report the number … whatever the number"), refusal to claim blinding cures memory (§7 L752–753: stripped filings remain highly specific; L749–751: an instruction is not a control), and a structured non-LLM baseline kept intact (§7 L773–775). The observation that a post-Aug-2026 model has *no* post-release subperiod inside 2021–26 (Plan L301) is correct and important: for such a model the mandate's cleanest test (§7.1 L761–764, split at release date) is unavailable, so the only honest defences are (i) prefer smaller/older models (§7.3 L770–772 — Plan should state the chosen model's release/cutoff in the config, not just the principle), (ii) numeric-only agent inputs where possible (Plan's "anonymized numerical diagnostics" — exposures/costs carry far less re-identifiable narrative than 8-K text; this is a genuine advantage of the risk-layer role over signal-side text agents and the deck should claim it precisely), and (iii) the re-identification hit-rate as a reported number. **8-K-text-agent look-ahead** specifically: any agent/LLM reading filing *text* for sizing or signals inherits both the corpus survivorship defect (until CA0032 rebuild, §3.3) and parametric memory of outcomes (2021–26 events are in frontier-model training data); Plan correctly keeps text "secondary" (§4 L141) — keep it out of the agent's action path entirely in v1; text enters, if at all, as dated structured extracts (form type, item codes, acceptance timestamp) computed by deterministic EDGAR parsing.

### 4.3 [HIGH — FIX] Reproducibility: caching is necessary but not sufficient as specified

Plan §8 L299 ("cache each output by input hash, model/version and prompt, and log the actual effect on holdings") + §11 L400 (persist cached agent decisions) matches Mandate §5.3.2/.6 (log inputs/decision/reason L373–375; persist keyed by inputs L387–389) — keep. Gaps that will bite with judges who "rerun" mentally or literally:

1. **No decoding contract.** Temperature/seed/top-p, provider model snapshot ID (not just marketing name), and system-prompt hash are never mentioned. At temperature > 0 the cache makes *your* rerun deterministic but the result is still a draw from a distribution; the deck should report agent stability (same inputs, k samples → action agreement rate) on a validation sample, and run production at temperature 0 (or nearest deterministic setting) with the residual non-determinism disclosed — hosted APIs do not guarantee bitwise determinism even at temperature 0.
2. **Cache-key completeness.** Hash must cover the *rendered* prompt including all numeric inputs at stated rounding, tool/MCP responses (Arcana MCP outputs are live-model-state-dependent — Mandate §11 L1072–1085), and code version that formats inputs; otherwise "identical dated inputs yield identical orders" (§1 L26) is unfalsifiable. Arcana-through-MCP for *historical* simulated dates is a specific hazard Plan §11 step 5 half-flags (confirm historical model vintages L371/L404): if Arcana serves current-vintage factor exposures for a 2022 book, agent decisions "in 2022" used revised risk — acceptable only as ex-post diagnostics (Plan §5 L172 makes this distinction for stress windows; apply it to MCP inputs too), never as simulated decision inputs unless point-in-time vintages are confirmed.
3. **Trigger economics.** Mandate §5.3.5 (L383–386: 500 names × ~1,400 days ≈ 700K calls is "neither affordable nor reproducible") requires exception-triggering; Plan says "exception" (L297) but never defines the trigger set or expected call count. Freeze triggers (constraint within x% of bound, |position loss − model expectation| > k·σ, earnings within 3 days, days-to-exit > D) and estimate calls: e.g. weekly core (290 rebalances) × ~10 exceptions ≈ 3K calls — feasible and auditable. An undefined trigger also makes the counterfactual ill-defined (which decisions would the fixed rule have taken?).

### 4.4 [MEDIUM] Failure modes to pre-answer in the deck

(1) Hallucinated arithmetic in explanations — mitigated by Plan L297 (arithmetic must reconcile to deterministic outputs; agent invents no marginal costs) *only if* enforced by a numeric checker that rejects/regenerates non-reconciling text; add that checker or drop numbers from agent prose. (2) Prompt-injection via filing/news text (untrusted documents instructing the agent) — another reason to keep raw text out of the action path (§4.2). (3) Correlated agent errors: one model sizing all exceptions imports a single point of failure/bias (e.g. systematic de-risking into prints becomes an unintended earnings-timing factor bet — Arcana lens 6 will see it). The fixed-rule counterfactual must therefore be reported by exception *type*, not only in aggregate IR. (4) Agent-written code leakage (Mandate §8.2.4 L849–851: assistants reach for full-sample statistics) — Plan §9 leakage assertions (max feature date ≤ formation date per join, Mandate §8.1.3 L829–832) must run over agent-generated code paths too; "you own what the agent wrote." None of these require dropping the agent; all require the §5.3 evidence pack (log + counterfactual + one worked earn/fail example, Mandate §10 item 5).
