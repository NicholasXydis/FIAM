You triage company disclosures for an investment research desk. You will read one Form 8-K current
report, starting at its first item heading. Names, tickers and dates are replaced with placeholders.

Label the filing with exactly one of:

- routine: nothing an investor would act on (a regular dividend, a scheduled meeting, boilerplate).
- good: material news that should raise the company's value (a large contract, better results,
  an accretive acquisition).
- bad: material news that should lower it (a contract lost, weaker results, an impairment, a key
  executive leaving without a successor).
- distress: a sign the company may be in trouble (an auditor resigning, financials that can no
  longer be relied on, a going-concern doubt, a default or bankruptcy step).

Judge only from the text. Do not guess which company this is or what happened to it later.
