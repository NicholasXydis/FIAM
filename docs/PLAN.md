### 3.3 Kahn (2018) additions, approved 2026-10-23
Kahn, *The Future of Investment Management* (CFA Institute Research Foundation), by the head of BlackRock's systematic equity research, who thanks Garvey. It is the jury's frame, so the deck uses it.
- **K1 Fundamental law.** IR = IC x √breadth x TC (Grinold 1989; Clarke-de Silva-Thorley 2002). Breadth is a rate: N times the forecast's information turnover, estimated by regressing each month's forecast on the last (Grinold-Kahn 2011), never names x 12. TC = correlation of the book with the unconstrained paper book. Exhibit on pre-2021 data with the implied IR; the same three numbers for 2021-2026 after the unseal. It replaces any typed return target.
- **K2 Research standard.** Every new signal must be sensible (a pre-2021 paper and a written rationale), predictive (its gate), consistent (IC by year reported) and additive (its gate removes SYY-10 and the 147). Plus one pre-written test of its mechanism that uses no returns (§7.3), reported and used as the live "still working" monitor, not a gate.
- **K3 Smart beta versus pure alpha.** Our Arcana pass reports the share of active variance explained by static style exposures and splits the rest into stock selection, industry and style timing. The momentum crash control's effect is labelled style timing.
- **K4 Realism.** IC 0.10 is "great" and is about 53% of signs right; a 90th-percentile long-short fund has IR about 1.17. A forecast IC above 0.10 or a pre-cost IR above 1.2 on pre-2021 or rehearsal books triggers a leak hunt (§14.2).
- **K5 Text.** Safe-harbour and other legal boilerplate is stripped before FinBERT tone and Lazy Prices similarity (step 4).
- **K6 Constraints.** Each self-imposed constraint (stricter than the PDF) gets its reason and its pre-2021 cost in TC and IR, reported, never tuned. Dropping even the smallest 80% of positions costs little TC, which supports 400-480 names and no-trade bands. The thesis cites Grossman-Stiglitz (1980). Red-team answer for "why no ESG scores": Garvey et al. (2017).

### 6.3 One clock for every merge (`fiam/v4/clock.py`, step 3)
- `available_at`: the instant the public could first see the row, stored in UTC, compared in America/New_York.
- `first_close`: for an event, the first trading close strictly after `available_at` (a stamp exactly at a close waits for the next); for market data, its own day's close (below); on one calendar: CRSP trading days 2000-2013, the organisers' dsf days from 2014 to 2026-09-28, each with its real close (16:00, or 13:00 on early-close days from the pinned `exchange_calendars` XNYS calendar, cross-checked against those days). Holidays and weekends roll forward. Rows outside the calendar keep a null `first_close` and are never joined.
- Market data (dsf and CRSP returns and prices, the panel's `eom` row) is available at its trading day's close, so its `first_close` is that close; the panel row uses the last trading day on or before `eom`.
- Event stamps (filings, events, news): a stamp before the close counts for that close; a stamp at or after the close counts for the next one. A date-only stamp means the end of that day, 23:59:59 America/New_York, so it counts for the next close; a month-end date-only row therefore joins the next `eom` (conservative, intended).
- The t+1 rule: information with `first_close` = d forms the book at the close of the next trading day, d+1, and first earns from d+1 to d+2 (PDF p15). So the `eom` 2020-12-31 row forms the first book, traded at the 2021-01-04 close (§9.1).
- Joins: daily on `(permno, first_close)`; monthly on `(permno, eom)` only when `first_close` is on or before that month's last trading day. Never on the observation date. Every join asserts that the latest `first_close` on the feature side is before the formation close (PDF p18); the engine's joins do so through `data.assert_public_by`.
- Thresholds (B21): any threshold that classifies a date (regime, percentile, trigger, screen) uses only data before that date (expanding window), in the pre-2021 tests and after.
- Short interest becomes available 8 trading days after settlement (one calendar everywhere).

### 6.4 Daily return rules (each counted and logged)
- Return basis: dsf `daily_return` = CRSP `DlyRetx` (price only) on all but 108 of 2,937,277 stock-days in 2015-2020 of stocks at $500m or more at the prior month end, where the dsf prices a spin-off's ex-day as a fall. Dividend term δ = (1 + chars `ret`) / Π(1 + dsf r) - 1 per name-month, only in months with every dsf session and no null, never in an exit month; a δ below -0.01% is set to 0 and logged, and every δ above 5% is logged (leader 2026-10-25; none below in 2015-2020); booked on the month's last trading day on the holding at the open of its ex-day; longs receive it, shorts pay it. Through 2025 CRSP dates the ex-day and its distributions are booked on their day, then trued up to δ; in 2026 a δ above 5% goes ex on the month's most negative dsf day (CRSP's day in 174 of 193 such stock-months of stocks at $500m or more at the prior month end in 2015-2020, 90.2%, and 111 of 115 above 10%; recounted 2026-10-25), a smaller one is paid on the month-end holding (approved 2026-10-25: rule A within 0.0055% of CRSP's exact days in every 2016-2020 month; the least-favourable rule B is run beside the headline as a robustness line, protocol "Engine"). Other months take CRSP `ret` minus `retx` through 2025, exit months included (only δ is barred there), plus a distribution the dsf missed; in 2026 none, and September 2026 has no panel month. It gives the total-return line, the headline or beside it per lever D3 (§4.2).
- Halts and missing days: a held name with no dsf row that day cannot trade and is held at its last value. On the 5th straight missing day it is written off at the stub (no trade assumed): -30% on longs, +10% against shorts (Shumway 1997). Halts are listed in the halt log. Through 2025 a held name whose dsf rows stop while CRSP still trades it (13 of $500m or more in 2015-2020, such as Mylan, Cboe and STERIS) is marked on CRSP's daily return and sold at the next formation, never stubbed (decision 2B); CRSP's delisting rows are never read as trades; a CRSP-marked name still held when CRSP ends closes at its last mark. A halt that resumes keeps its stub: of the 5 such gaps of $500m+ stocks in 2015-2020 (98-501 sessions) 4 are stubs and Interactive Brokers is marked on CRSP; none occur in 2021-2026 (counts only).
- Stubs are trued up: through 2025 to the CRSP delisting return or the panel's exit-month residual against CRSP's daily total return, once that panel row is public (a short keeps its stub on a failure, never gaining from the panel's -30% fill); in 2026, exits dated by the first SEC exit form on or after the last row (Forms 25 and 15, 8-K items 2.01 and 1.03), mergers at the last trade, failures keep the stub (A12, A13, B8). Stub log by year and leg and the halt log are written per run; robustness run adverse both ways.
- The dated exits table `data/v4/clean/exits.parquet` ([DONE 2026-10-23] `fiam/v4/exits.py`: 14,618 CRSP exits; of 178 stocks stopping in 2026 or on 2025-12-31 with no CRSP exit, 117 mergers, 9 delisted, 6 bankruptcies and 46 data gaps; rebuilt 2026-10-25 with 2 bankruptcies under Low 7 and each 2026 exit booked from its first exit form after the stop, a median 2 sessions after it, `out/v4/engine/exits_diff.json`):
  - through 2025, CRSP delistings by CIZ action, read from payments and returns: MER merger and GEX exchange (merger or exchange), GLI liquidation and GDR dropped (failure)
  - in 2026, a name whose dsf rows stop is an exit only if a Form 25, a Form 15 or an 8-K with item 2.01 or 1.03 is filed within 30 trading days either side of its last row (forms from `tools/data/edgar.py forms`, §11), and so is a stock stopping on 2025-12-31 with no CRSP exit: a failure if item 1.03 is among them or was filed in the 12 months before the stop (M5, Low 7), else a merger if item 2.01 is, else a failure; otherwise a data gap, closed at its last dsf price, never stubbed, listed by name with the stub result beside (Cadence Bank, acquired in 2026, files with bank regulators; decision 2B)
  - dropping out of the panel alone is never an exit (the organisers' `readme.md` §7); Compustat `dldte`/`dlrsn` enter only here, counted from their own dates (B11)
- Prices: dsf `prc` is split-adjusted to today and holds `inf` (permno 12350 to 2024-09-16); banned except for the gap ratio below (§1.2). Cap = chars `me` at the last `eom` x cumulative dsf return since then. Nulls (6,550): a first-row null cannot be held; after a gap use the price ratio if finite, else 0, flagged. Sub-penny 2026 glitches (727 rows on 17 stocks, permno 21298 on 2026-09-08 = 2999 among them) never touch a $500m and $5 stock (asserted, counts only). Held \|r\| > 50% is listed beside chars `ret` (`big_moves.csv`), never edited.

## 7. Signals

### 7.0 Thesis and when it stops (written 2026-10-23, before any v4 run)
- Thesis: investors underreact to slow, complex or costly-to-read information about a firm's real business: accounting and investment quality (the SYY anomalies), disclosures whose wording changes, red-flag filings, what informed insiders, the best fund managers and short sellers do, and news that travels along customer links. Limited attention and short-sale frictions delay the response; information that is costly to process stays partly unpriced (Grossman-Stiglitz 1980), which is where pure alpha lives (Kahn 2018). In large caps those frictions are smaller, so the edge must be residual, not factor tilts (Arcana lens 1).
- When it stops: crowding or post-publication decay (McLean-Pontiff); momentum crashes after rebounds (Daniel-Moskowitz); low cross-sectional dispersion; a liquidity crisis forcing correlated deleveraging (August 2007); style rotations swamping stock-level information. Each has a kill or brake rule (§13.5, §8.3, §8.4) and a slide.
- Edge levers (2026-10-23): the residual target (§8.2); signals combined by the ML as monotone inputs with fixed signs, never hand-set weights; the forecast aligned with the constraints (lever C1); cost and turnover discipline (no-trade bands, the frozen cost budget, the no-trade rule, patient execution); the daily overlays if their decay tests pass (lever E5, §8.7); no further signals.

## Appendix B. Glossary
- **Merge rules** (the organisers' workbook, sheet "3. Merge rules"): 1 "Shift every series by its published lag", merge on the availability date; 2 "Treat the data as current-vintage, not point-in-time"; 3 "Link on PERMNO with a historical ticker map"; 4 "Never trade the 'has data' flag"; 5 "Size the sleeve to the coverage" (names with data per month, share of universe and of gross); 6 "Check the start date against the data, not the label" (row counts by month).
- **Residual return**: the label after the §8.2 regression. **Residual IC**: Spearman correlation of a signal with it (§7.4). **IC, breadth, TC**: skill, independent bets per year, and correlation of the book with the unconstrained paper book (K1).
- **Factor share**: the share of the book's ex-ante variance from the style factors (§8.4). **Brake**: v3's drawdown brake; its v4 form is fixed in step 4.
- **Chance band**: the 95% binomial band of guessing the company or month at random (§10.4). **Stub**: the provisional return booked when a name disappears (§6.4).
- **Current vintage**: a series as it stands today, possibly revised since (PDF p15). **Formation**: the monthly decision and its spill days; **first_close**: §6.3. **CURRENT.json**: the run hash every site and deck input carries.
- **Arms A0, A1, A2, P1**: no desk, fixed rule, agent, random placebo (§10.4), always written "arm A1". **Levers**: the metric and design choices of §8.8, always written "lever A2"; a bare A1-A17 or B1-B31 is an audit fix (§6.8).
- **Crash-day cap C_t**: 200% / (1 + m_t), m_t the largest two-day rise seen before t (lever A2). **Live**: the final 2026-08-31 book and event-day use only; never a backtested card, never a scored month (the September book is frozen).
- **Best idea**: a 13F filer's holding with the largest weight above its market weight (§7.3). **Opportunistic insider**: one with no same-month trades in each of the three prior years (§7.3). **Churn rate**: how much of a 13F portfolio turns over in a quarter; the top third are short-horizon institutions (lever C3). **Practice exam**: lever E1's pre-2021 comparison of fourteen designs (§8.9).
